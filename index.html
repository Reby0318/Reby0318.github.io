01

The through-line

Most people in the music industry are strong in one of three areas: they understand the music, they understand the business, or they understand the data. Decisions get made at the seams between them — and that is where the value leaks out.

My background sits across all three. Conservatory training taught me how music is actually made and what it costs an artist to make it. Six years of running events, enrollment and communications taught me how the organisations around that artist actually operate. An MBA is teaching me how they make money. And analytics is how I turn all of it into evidence instead of opinion.

Practically, that means I am the person who can look at a chart dataset, spot that the metric everyone relies on has stopped working, explain why in commercial terms, and then build the dashboard that lets a team act on it — without needing three people to translate between each step.

Signal vs. Noise

Published

Quantifying the discovery bottleneck in the streaming era

The music industry's binding constraint has flipped from supply to verification: 106,000 tracks now arrive daily, more than half of Deezer's uploads are AI-generated, and streaming fraud drains roughly $2bn a year. Nobody can cheaply tell durable demand from manufactured attention.

I analysed 68 years of Billboard Hot 100 data — 355,087 song-weeks — to measure how far the industry's demand signal has degraded, then tested which early metric actually predicts a hit's commercial life. The answer overturns standard practice: debut chart position is close to noise, while week-eight retention predicts durability 3.5× better — early enough to reallocate marketing spend.
