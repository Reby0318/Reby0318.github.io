01

The through-line

Most people in the music industry are strong in one of three areas: they understand the music, they understand the business, or they understand the data. Decisions get made at the seams between them — and that is where the value leaks out.

My background sits across all three. Years of conservatory training taught me how music is actually made and what it costs an artist to make it. An MBA is teaching me how the companies around that artist make money. And analytics is how I turn the two into evidence instead of opinion.

Practically, that means I am the person who can look at a chart dataset, spot that the metric everyone relies on has stopped working, explain why in commercial terms, and then build the dashboard that lets a team act on it — without needing three people to translate between each step.

Signal vs. Noise

Published

Quantifying the discovery bottleneck in the streaming era

The music industry's binding constraint has flipped from supply to verification: 106,000 tracks now arrive daily, more than half of Deezer's uploads are AI-generated, and streaming fraud drains roughly $2bn a year. Nobody can cheaply tell durable demand from manufactured attention.

I analysed 68 years of Billboard Hot 100 data — 355,087 song-weeks — to measure how far the industry's demand signal has degraded, then tested which early metric actually predicts a hit's commercial life. The answer overturns standard practice: debut chart position is close to noise, while week-eight retention predicts durability 3.5× better — early enough to reallocate marketing spend.

Global Repertoire Shift

In progress

Where local music is winning, and what that means for catalog strategy

Latin America grew 17.1% in 2025, MENA and Sub-Saharan Africa 15.2% each — the industry's growth has moved decisively outside its traditional markets. Using daily Spotify chart data across 73 countries, this project measures how fast local repertoire is displacing international repertoire market by market, and which markets are still open to catalog from elsewhere.

The Superfan Model

Planned

Pricing a tier the industry has been promising for three years

Roughly 2% of an artist's listeners drive 18% of their streams, yet Spotify's superfan tier has stalled repeatedly on what it should contain. This project builds a scenario model in Excel — conversion rate against price point against incremental ARPU — to show which combinations actually clear the revenue targets the majors have publicly assumed, and which do not.

What licensed AI music does to the value of a back catalog

With Warner settling with Suno and Universal with Udio, catalog valuation for AI training is now live commercial work. This project tracks disclosed AI-content share against consumption data to test a straightforward question: does an AI-saturated genre lose measurable value in its human catalog?
