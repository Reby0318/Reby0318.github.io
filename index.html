Signal vs. Noise

Published

Quantifying the discovery bottleneck in the streaming era

The music industry's binding constraint has flipped from supply to verification: 106,000 tracks now arrive daily, more than half of Deezer's uploads are AI-generated, and streaming fraud drains roughly $2bn a year. Nobody can cheaply tell durable demand from manufactured attention.

I analysed 68 years of Billboard Hot 100 data — 355,087 song-weeks — to measure how far the industry's demand signal has degraded, then tested which early metric actually predicts a hit's commercial life. The result runs against standard practice: debut chart position predicts very little, while position at week eight predicts durability about 3.5× better — early enough to still act on.

No Global Charts

Published

Where local music wins, and what that means for release strategy

The industry plans releases as though music were borderless. I collected a single day's Spotify charts across 72 countries — 14,049 chart rows — and matched tracks by Spotify ID so the same song is recognised across any language or script.

81.4% of charting tracks appear in exactly one country, and not one of the 72 markets draws a majority of its chart from globally shared music. Markets are either self-supplying (Turkey 98%, Japan 91%) or supplied by a language bloc — El Salvador charts almost nothing unique, but only 5% of its chart is global; the rest is Spanish-language repertoire shared across Latin America.
